What counts as a good score — raw scores are absolute; the labels are graded on a bell curve relative to this run

Tavily score · 0–1

Raw relevance from Tavily's own ranking. 0.7+ is strong. Articles routed in by Telem have no Tavily score (shown as n/a) — the formula uses 0.5 for those.

strong ≥ 0.7

Mantra grounding · 0–1

Share of the query's words that appear in the article — the original prompt repeated against each article. 0.7+ is strong. Note: it saturates at 1.0 on broad queries (every top article contains all the query words), so it discriminates best on narrow queries.

strong ≥ 0.7

Combined relevance · 0–1

0.6 × Tavily + 0.4 × mantra grounding. Reliability badge: High ≥ 0.6, Medium ≥ 0.35, Low < 0.35. 0.75+ is strong.

Medium ≥ 0.35
High ≥ 0.6
strong ≥ 0.75

Redundancy (max Jaccard) · 0–1 — lower is better

Highest token overlap with any other article in the run. Any pair above 0.05 pays a QUBO penalty of 1.2 × similarity — that's how near-duplicates get pushed out of the best set.

penalty > 0.05

Net score — the one combined number

relevance − redundancy + meta bonus + wave bonus. Meta bonus +0.05 when an article is found by both a wave round and the meta pass (opposite profile); wave bonus +0.01 per extra distinct wave round, max +0.05. High relevance, low overlap, cross-validated. This is what the Top 10 is ranked by — and the same combined signal the annealer optimizes, so the two lists now largely agree.

Bell-curve grading — labels are relative to this run's distribution

Each label comes from the article's z-score: how many standard deviations it sits from this run's mean for that metric. super frickin' amazing ≥ +1σ · amazing +0.5σ to +1σ · good 0 to +0.5σ · medium −0.5σ to 0 · medium rare −1σ to −0.5σ · really frickin' bad < −1σ. For redundancy the scale is inverted (lower is better). A tier here means “ranked that way compared with the other articles in this run” — the raw score next to it is the absolute number. (Not the same as the reliability badge High / Medium / Low, which is an absolute cutoff on relevance.)

Convergence: how many of the 100 annealing reads landed on the optimum. 100/100 is ideal on small sets; lower at scale is expected — our 111-article run converged 31/100. QUBO: hi = 0.45 − relevancei, Jij = 1.2 · Jaccard(i,j) when similarity > 0.05, solved with 100 reads of local simulated annealing.
